# The Moon Is a Planet You Can Actually Feel. Here's How to Track It

*Twenty-nine days, eight phases, one very visible cycle.*

The Moon is the one object in the sky almost everyone can feel — tides, night walks, a string of dark evenings before the new moon. Yet most people never know what phase it's in, or why it matters to the sky they live under.

**The cycle in plain terms**

The Moon takes about 29.5 days to circle Earth and return to the same phase. New Moon (dark) → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → Full Moon → waning gibbous → last quarter → waning crescent, then back to new. Half the cycle is "waxing" (light grows), half is "waning" (light shrinks).

**What's actually happening**

The illuminated fraction you see is the geometry of sunlight on a sphere. When the Moon sits between Earth and Sun, you see the dark side (new). When Earth is the middle of the sandwich, you see the fully lit face (full). Nothing in the Moon changes — just the angle.

**Why tracking it is worth it**

Knowing the phase tells you, in a calm, factual way, where you are in a monthly rhythm: dark, growing, full, releasing. Farmers used it for planting; modern stargazers use it to plan observation nights (full moon = bad for faint stars, great for exploring the Moon itself).
