# Synastry, Plainly: What Compatibility Charts Actually Compare

Synastry sounds intimidating, but it's a simple operation: take two birth charts and compare the planet positions from each. "Their Mars is on my Sun" or "her Moon squares his Saturn" — synastry is the study of those intersections, called interaspects, and how they form the famous sparks, frictions, or quiet easiness between two people.

**What actually gets compared**

The meaningful grid is small. You compare each person's Sun, Moon, rising, and a handful of personal planets against the other's, then read the angles formed (conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition). The Sun–Moon and Moon–Moon pairings carry most of the emotional weight; the outer planets add context but move slowly and describe the generation, not the couple.

**The honest caveat**

A birth chart can't predict whether a relationship survives — real compatibility includes trust, timing, and how people handle conflict, none of which live in an ephemeris. What synastry is genuinely good at is *naming the pattern*: recognizing why two people feel instantly familiar, or where recurring friction tends to come from. That vocabulary is useful in real life.

**How to treat the result**

Use the interaspects as a conversation starter, not a scorecard. If you and someone read the same Venus–Sun trine, notice it; if you hit a Mars–Saturn square, expect the power tussles it describes and plan for them. The planets are exact, the meaning is a lens — and a good lens helps you look, not replace what you see.
