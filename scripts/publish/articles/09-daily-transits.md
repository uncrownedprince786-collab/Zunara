# Daily Transits, Decoded: Why Your Horoscope Changes Every Day

When an astrologer says "today's transits," they don't mean a vibe. They mean a precise measurement: the sky's *current* planetary positions, each compared against the positions in your birth chart. A transit happens the moment today's Sun, Moon or a moving planet makes an aspect to something in your chart. That's a computed event, not a mood.

**What moves day to day**

The Moon circles the sky in about twenty-eight days, so its transit against your chart changes every couple of days — that's the heartbeat of a daily reading. The Sun and planets move slower: a single Mercury or Venus transit can last days or weeks. So your horoscope isn't random; it reflects which transits are literally active *right now*, and the astronomy behind them is real and verifiable.

**Why it changes so often**

Because your chart is fixed but the sky isn't. On the day you were born, the planets stopped for a snapshot. Every day since, they've kept moving, forming new angles against that frozen frame. A daily horoscope is just a report of the newest alignments. The more moving bodies in the sky, the richer the daily ups and downs.

**The healthy mental model**

Transits are a calendar of influences, not instructions. Use them as reflection prompts: on a tense Saturn transit, expect friction with authority and planning; on a sunny Jupiter transit, notice when growth gets a tailwind. The positions are exact to a fraction of a degree; what you do with the symbolism is your choice — and knowing *why* the reading changed makes it a tool, not a rule.
