# What Famous Birthdays Tell Us About Charts — and Confirmation Bias

Look up the charts of a dozen famous people and they *feel* like they match the biographies: the driven Capricorn, the magnetically charismatic Pisces. It's convincing — and it's mostly confirmation bias. You don't get compliments on the twelve planets that don't line up with the story.

**The survivorship problem**

You start with famous people — survivors of brutal selection. Of course traits like drive, assertiveness and luck-adjacent timing show up in their charts at higher rates than a random room of ten people. You're reading a chart of people who already made it, not a chart of people.

**The selection-after-the-fact trick**

If you pick a trait you *know* is famous ("a perfectionist, workaholic, secretly anxious") and then scan ten planets for evidence, you'll always find it. With Sun, Moon, rising, Mars, Saturn and nodes, that's six surfaces to project a story onto. The chart is a Rorschach that always agrees with you — because you only report the hits.

**The honest use**

Birth charts are still worth studying, but as a map of *tendencies and timing*, not a fortune cookie. Use them to notice repeating patterns in your own life, set reflection points around transits, and understand *why you react the way you do* — not to predict whether you'll become famous. The astronomy is exact; what you do with the symbolism is your choice.
